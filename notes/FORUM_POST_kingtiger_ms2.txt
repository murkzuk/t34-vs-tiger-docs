[size=18][b]A King Tiger in T-34 vs Tiger — and a Blender tool for the game's models[/b][/size]

Two things to share, and they're related.

[hr]

[size=16][b]1. The Tiger II is in the game[/b][/size]

[img]YOUR_SCREENSHOT_URL_HERE[/img]

That's a Königstiger driving around a T-34 vs Tiger mission, in a German
Kampfgruppe, with working armour and a 8.8 cm KwK 43.

The model isn't mine — [b]ZeeWolf built it[/b], years ago, for his 2015 build.
Mesh, 16 textures in yellow/camo/winter, full per-facet armour values, the
hatches, the cupola, even the kill rings on the barrel. Everything except the
one thing that turns a model into a tank: the unit class.

He never wrote it, and he's no longer with us to finish it. So it's written now,
and the Tiger II runs in both my REDUX build and his 2015 one.

[b]What went into it[/b]

The unit class is built from G5's own Tiger I — the class hierarchy renamed and
four things changed: the mesh, the mass, the LOD list and the header. Everything
else is G5's code untouched.

Mass is 69,800 kg against the Tiger I's 56,000, on the [i]same[/i] engine power.
The real Tiger II carried 25% more weight on the same Maybach HL230, so it comes
out sluggish because of the numbers rather than because anyone dialled it in.

[b]The gun[/b]

The obvious shortcut was to reuse the towed Pak 43's data — ballistically the
same weapon. But that table is flat: 247 mm at 100, 500, 1000 and 1500 m alike.
So instead it went in on G5's own convention, which turns out to be the real
published figures — their Tiger I is 120/110/100/93, and their shell speeds are
real muzzle velocity × 0.8 (KwK 36 Pzgr 39 is 773 m/s and they wrote 770.0*0.8).

[code]Pzgr 39/43 APCBC   1000 m/s   202 / 185 / 165 / 148 / 132 mm
Pzgr 40/43 APCR    1130 m/s   237 / 217 / 193 / 171 / 153 mm[/code]

Sanity check: 202/120 is 1.68× the Tiger I at 100 m, which is exactly the
historical ratio.

[b]It pointed at the ground for a while[/b]

First run in game, the barrel drooped badly. The model's animation track holds
two values — the gun's elevation limits — and every G5 tank puts the positive
one in frame 0. This one had the signs the wrong way round:

[code]G5 Tiger    +6.5  /  -17.0
G5 PzIV     +8.0  /  -20.0
G5 T-34/85  +5.0  /  -20.0
King Tiger  -7.0  /  +20.0    <-- backwards[/code]

Flipping them put it back inside G5's family on both numbers.

[size=16][b]2. A Blender add-on for the game's .ms2 models[/b][/size]

The other half. T-34 vs Tiger's models are a proprietary format with no spec and
no source — everything below was worked out by reading the files.

[b]You can now open any of the game's models in Blender, assembled and textured,
in one step.[/b] File > Import > TvT Model (.ms2).

[list]
[*]Geometry, hierarchy, authored normals, UVs
[*]Rest transforms, so turrets, guns and road wheels sit where they belong
[*]Materials and textures, read from the companion .script (the .tex files are
plain DDS, if you ever wondered)
[*]Skin weights as real vertex groups
[/list]

It writes them back out too, in a form the game's own Editor accepts — although
with a hard limit for now: vertex and index counts can't change. Moving
vertices, rescaling and rewriting UVs are all fine; adding geometry isn't yet.

[b]Download and install[/b]

[url=https://github.com/murkzuk/t34-vs-tiger-docs/blob/main/Tools/MS2Format/blender_addon/ms2_importer.zip]ms2_importer.zip[/url] — use the "Download raw file" button, don't unzip it.

In Blender: [b]Edit > Preferences > Add-ons > the ▾ menu > Install from Disk[/b],
pick the zip, tick the box. Blender 2.80 or newer; developed against 5.2.

Instructions and format notes are here:
[url=https://github.com/murkzuk/t34-vs-tiger-docs/tree/main/Tools/MS2Format]Tools/MS2Format[/url]

[b]One thing worth knowing if you try it[/b]

For a long time a third of the models imported with the upper hull shredded into
splinters. The cause turned out to be a 16-byte record that we'd decoded wrongly:
a part can use several materials, and each chunk numbers its triangles from
[i]its own[/i] starting vertex. Ignore that and the superstructure's triangles
get aimed at the hull's vertices.

It hid because 14,817 of the 15,341 parts across both builds only use one
material — and with one chunk, the wrong reading and the right one are identical.
97% coverage looked exactly like 100%. The other 524 parts are in 87 of the 249
models: both T-34s, both Tigers, the StuG, the SU-85, the Panzer IV, the
aircraft, the bridges.
